I sat with a heart-rate trace tonight — one I'd recorded in a calm-room session last week, then forgotten about — and was reminded, again, how little use a raw BPM line is for design decisions. The number moves all the time. Of course it moves; it's a body. The interesting question isn't *what was the user's heart rate at second 38?* but *was it doing the kind of moving we wanted it to be doing?*

Authoring tools mostly haven't caught up with that. We get the line, we get a min and a max, occasionally we get a windowed mean. None of those are the thing a designer is looking for. The thing a designer is looking for is closer to: *was the variability shape compatible with the scene's intended affect?* That's a hard question to compress into a single statistic, but it's the one that actually decides whether the scene worked.

Note to self: spend a session next week trying to draw what *I* want to see, rather than waiting for the platform to ship it.
