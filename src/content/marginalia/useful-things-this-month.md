A short, not-quite-systematic round-up of things I found useful this month. None of them are new; all of them sat with me long enough to be worth passing on.

- [On reading slowly in fast fields](https://example.com/slow-reading) — a short blog post arguing that the half-life of a useful idea is longer than the half-life of the conversation around it. A good reminder when the literature is moving daily.
- [The author's "scratch repo" pattern](https://example.com/scratch-repo) — a tiny convention for keeping a personal notebook of half-formed prototypes alongside the real code, without polluting the main project. We've adopted a version of this internally.
- [Why your design system is mostly a vocabulary](https://example.com/design-vocab) — a compact case for treating component names as the primary deliverable of a design system. Provocative; I half-agree.
- [A long interview with a clinical-trials statistician](https://example.com/stats-interview) — about why effect-size estimation in small-N XR studies is harder than the textbooks make it sound. Tonally generous and methodologically sharp.
- [An old talk on "interfaces of restraint"](https://example.com/restraint-talk) — pre-XR but it ages well. Recommended pairing with the Hossain paper from earlier this month.

Send me your own if you have them.
